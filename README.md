# Can Cannabis sativa seed morphometrics discriminate among variety, sex, and chemotype of the adult plant?

This repository contains the data used in the study of seed morphology as a biomarker for *Cannabis sativa* L. varieties.

## 📝 Abstract 
**Background:** Cannabis sativa L. has been cultivated and domesticated by humans since ancient times for multiple purposes, resulting in extensive genetic and phenotypic diversity. Based on cannabinoid composition, at least three major chemotypes can be distinguished: type I (>THC), type II (THC:CBD), and type III (>CBD). The identification of early morphological biomarkers associated with varietal identity, chemotype, and sex would provide valuable tools for breeding programs and quality control. This study evaluated the usefulness of regular seed shape as an early biomarker for variety, sex, and chemotype in two Patagonian C. sativa varieties with therapeutic potential.
**Methods:** Two varieties were analyzed: “Néstor”, derived from a cross between two type I parental lines, and “Milstein”, obtained from a cross between type I and type III parental lines. Seed morphology was characterized using 2D geometric morphometrics based on landmarks and semilandmarks combined with multivariate statistical analyses. Chemotypic characterization was performed by growing plants from individual seeds, harvesting female inflorescences, and analyzing cannabinoid profiles by thin-layer chromatography.
**Results:** Seeds of the Néstor variety (type I-THC) were significantly larger and displayed a rounder shape, whereas Milstein seeds (type I-THC and type II-THC:CBD) were smaller and tended toward an oval-elongated morphology. Discriminant function analysis based on seed shape correctly classified 100% of Néstor and 95% of Milstein seeds. No significant sexual dimorphism was detected in seed size or shape within either variety. Likewise, no statistically significant differences were observed between type I and type II chemotypes when grouped by seed morphology.
**Conclusions:** Seed size and shape constitutes a reliable early biomarker for varietal identification and may serve as a practical indicator of seed quality and authenticity in regular Cannabis sativa varieties. However, it is not suitable for discriminating sex or non-contrasting chemotypes in unstabilized varieties such as Milstein. These findings support the application of geometric morphometric approaches in cannabis breeding and varietal authentication programs.

## 📁 Repository Structure
